Methodology / v1

Evidence before prediction.

AI Reset Watch is an independent, unaffiliated record of public usage-reset and quota-policy announcements, plus direct measurements of one operator-owned Codex account. This page explains what each kind of evidence can—and cannot—support.

What counts as a signal

An event must describe a usage reset, a temporary quota intervention, or a durable quota-policy change affecting Claude Code, Codex/ChatGPT, Gemini CLI, or Code Assist. Product launches and unrelated status incidents are excluded.

Times represent the linked public announcement or the closest observable publication time. They are not internal provider execution timestamps. Events labeled “approximate” have date-level uncertainty and should not be used for hour-of-day analysis.

Own-account observations are a separate kind of row with separate rules. They are described in Natural expiry or an actual reset and never enter the announcement counts, the “last tracked move” figure, or the weekday timing pattern.

Sources and coverage

  • OpenAI: refreshed from the public codex-resets.com feed, with event links retained to the original public posts. The upstream feed is a secondary aggregation source.
  • Anthropic: manually curated from @ClaudeDevs. Coverage may be incomplete; uncertain dates carry an approximate-confidence label.
  • Google: manually curated from official Google and Google Developers announcements. These are generally durable policy changes rather than recurring resets.

The OpenAI feed is refreshed about every five minutes. Anthropic and Google updates are curated less frequently. A missing event does not prove that no provider-side action occurred.

Account recovery is not a provider-wide reset

Our probes watch the operator's Codex and Claude Code quota windows. A counter clearing early tells us when this account recovered quota, but it does not tell us who caused it or which other users were affected.

Scheduled expiry
The clear landed at or after the scheduled expiry recorded while the window was active. We may show it as an observation of our own account; it never counts as an extra provider reset.
Personal reset use
A Bank count falling across a clear suggests personal redemption, but credits can also expire. These observations stay private and never confirm a public reset.
Cause unverified
An early clear, or a clear without a recorded expiry anchor, stays in the private probe log. An unchanged Bank count does not exclude a grant and redemption between polls. Multiple accounts clearing together does not establish the cause either.
Bank credits granted
A credit becoming available is separate from using it. An official announcement granting reset credits does not mean users' quota windows have already reset.

OpenAI documents separate Bank balances and redemption operations. Claude personal limit resets can restore five-hour or weekly limits, including the same account's Claude Code usage. Our Claude usage response does not expose a reset-redemption ledger.

Account-only clears never mark an announcement confirmed, originate a public reset, or enter public reset counts. The same rule applies when old probe logs are exported again. Official announcements remain visible with the scope stated by their source; an announcement and a nearby account clear alone do not prove causation.

What the probes can establish

  • Readings, not fleet coverage. The probes measure one Codex account and two Claude accounts. Google has no active probe. A freshness badge describes the last successful reading, not verification of a provider-wide reset.
  • Detection limits. Windows with less than a 10-point usage drop may not show a detectable clear. Missing readings mean unknown, not that nothing happened.
  • Arrival timing. A clear is bracketed by the readings either side of it. That interval is not the time a provider started or completed a rollout.
  • No redemption during monitoring. Probes read quota metadata without spending reset credits.

Public observations and probe freshness are available in data.json. Personal and ambiguous clears remain outside that feed.

Forecast language

The site does not publish a single predicted reset date. Backtests found that point estimates implied more precision than the history supports. Where enough gaps exist, the interface instead reports an empirical conditional rate: among historical gaps already at least as long as the current wait, what fraction ended within the following seven days?

Small comparison sets are labeled. Historical frequency is context, not a promise, causal model, or provider commitment.

Weekday and time-of-day patterns

Codex events with exact timestamps are converted to America/Los_Angeles, including daylight-saving changes, and grouped into four six-hour periods: overnight (00:00–05:59), morning (06:00–11:59), afternoon (12:00–17:59), and evening (18:00–23:59).

The “last 30 days” callout is compared with the complete tracked history. It is descriptive only. Repeated posts may be related, event discovery may be incomplete, and choosing a pattern after seeing the data can exaggerate apparent regularity. The interface therefore calls recent Friday concentration a weak observation rather than a routine.

Machine-readable access and attribution

The complete merged feed is published as JSON. Each event includes a vendor-scoped identifier, announcement time, text, event kind, confidence where applicable, and a source URL. A provider with an own-account probe also carries a probe block (status, last verified time, coverage sentence) and an observations list; only observations cleared for publication appear there. The homepage also publishes Dataset structured data and a crawlable snapshot of current signals.

When citing this tracker, link both AI Reset Watch and the event’s original source. Do not describe this independent aggregation as an official provider record.

Frequently asked questions

Does the site know when provider systems actually reset?

Only for one account. A probe on one Codex Pro account measures that account’s own usage windows and can say whether a window expired on schedule or cleared early with no explanation on our side. Everything else is public announcements. No provider telemetry is visible to this site, and no observation here describes any account but ours.

Are timing patterns predictions?

No. They summarize tracked announcement timestamps and are explicitly limited by sample size and coverage.

Is this affiliated with Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google?

No. AI Reset Watch is independently maintained and unaffiliated.
