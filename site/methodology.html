Methodology / v1

Evidence before prediction.

AI Reset Watch is an independent, unaffiliated record of public usage-reset and quota-policy announcements, plus direct measurements of the operator’s own Codex and Claude Code accounts. This page explains what each kind of evidence can—and cannot—support.

What counts as a signal

An event must describe a usage reset, a temporary quota intervention, or a durable quota-policy change affecting Claude Code, Codex/ChatGPT, Gemini CLI, or Code Assist. Product launches and unrelated status incidents are excluded.

Times represent the linked public announcement or the closest observable publication time. They are not internal provider execution timestamps. Events labeled “approximate” have date-level uncertainty and should not be used for hour-of-day analysis.

Own-account observations are a separate kind of row with separate rules. They are described in Account recovery is not a provider-wide reset and never enter the announcement counts, the “last tracked move” figure, or the weekday timing pattern.

Sources and coverage

  • OpenAI: refreshed from the public codex-resets.com feed, with event links retained to the original public posts. The upstream feed is a secondary aggregation source.
  • Anthropic: refreshed from the public claude-resets.com feed, which is itself curated by hand, plus posts added here by hand when it misses one. Each post’s wording is checked against the original on X where it can be. Coverage may be incomplete; uncertain dates carry an approximate-confidence label.
  • Google: manually curated from official Google and Google Developers announcements. These are generally durable policy changes rather than recurring resets.

The OpenAI and Anthropic feeds are refreshed about every five minutes. Google updates are curated less frequently. A missing event does not prove that no provider-side action occurred.

Account recovery is not a provider-wide reset

Our probes read the usage windows of Claude Code and Codex accounts that the operator holds. Each provider’s coverage sentence names the accounts and windows being read now: it is in that provider’s probe block in data.json, and under its own-account readings on the homepage whenever there are any. When a window that was in use drops to zero, the probe knows when that happened on that account. It does not know who caused it, or which other users were affected.

What one account can rule out

Before an early clear counts for anything, the probe checks every cause on the account holder’s side that it can see:

  • A switch of account or plan. Each reading records a one-way fingerprint of the signed-in account, and the plan. A clear across a switch is two different quotas, not a reset.
  • A banked reset spent. Codex accounts can save reset credits and redeem them from any device. The probe reads the bank every minute, and a credit leaving it just before or during the clear makes the clear the account holder’s own, even though a credit can also simply expire. When the response lists each credit, the probe can also tell a credit spent early from one that expired on schedule. When it gives only a count, a published row says only what the count read, because a credit granted and a credit spent between two readings cancel out in a count.
  • The Claude Code reset menu. If the menu was opened on this machine in the hour before a Claude clear, the clear is the account holder’s own.
  • A gap in the readings. If the readings either side of a clear are more than three polls apart, there was room for something the probe never saw, so its cause stays open.

What one account cannot see

Two ways of resetting a limit leave nothing a probe can read. OpenAI sells personal plans an instant reset that touches neither the bank nor anything else the usage response reports. A Claude limit reset redeemed on the web or in the desktop app keeps the weekly schedule, exactly as a provider reset does. So an early clear with everything above ruled out still looks the same whether the provider reset everyone or the account holder paid to reset their own. One account is never enough.

The second witness

An early clear counts as the provider’s reset only when something independent of that account agrees:

Another of our accounts
A second, separate account of ours, with the same checks passed, cleared the same main weekly window within an hour: the weekly limit for Codex, the all-models weekly limit for Claude. Narrower weekly limits that clear with it, such as a per-model limit, never form a pair of their own. Both clears are shown, each naming the other as its witness. If both were at 95% or more of their limit beforehand, they stay private: that is exactly when an account holder would pay to reset. While only one of our accounts is read for a provider, this witness cannot occur there, and its coverage sentence names one account.
The provider’s own post
A verified post from a reviewed official or staff account says a reset already happened, and it was posted between one hour before and three hours after the clear. The announcement then carries one line saying when our account saw the clear, relative to the post: “Seen on our account 2 min before this post”. The single account’s clear is not shown as a row of its own.

How each kind of clear is shown

Scheduled expiry
The clear landed at the scheduled expiry recorded while the window was active. We may show it as an observation of our own account; it never counts as an extra provider reset.
Reset on two of our accounts
Shown beside the other account’s clear. With no announcement yet, it may start an alert after a two-hour hold. If either clear later reverts, with two readings in a row showing the old usage back, both rows leave the page, an alert still waiting is cancelled, and subscribers already alerted are told we withdrew it, unless they turned that follow-up off.
Our own reset, or a switch of account
Kept in the private probe log. It describes what the account holder did, not what the provider did.
Cause not established
An early clear on one account stays in the private probe log, whether every visible cause was ruled out or a check could not be run. So does a clear with no recorded expiry, and every five-hour Claude clear, which ordinary expiry and the reset command produce alike.
Bank credits granted
A credit becoming available is separate from using it. An official announcement granting reset credits does not mean users’ quota windows have already reset.

OpenAI documents separate Bank balances and redemption operations. Claude personal limit resets can restore five-hour or weekly limits, including the same account’s Claude Code usage. Our Claude usage response does not expose a reset-redemption ledger.

Old probe logs carry no account fingerprint, so exporting them again can never turn one of their clears into a witness of either kind. Official announcements remain visible with the scope stated by their source.

What the probes can establish

  • Readings, not fleet coverage. The probes measure only the operator’s own Claude Code and Codex accounts, and each provider’s coverage sentence says which. Google has no active probe. A freshness badge describes the last successful reading, not verification of a provider-wide reset.
  • Detection limits. Windows with less than a 10-point usage drop may not show a detectable clear. Missing readings mean unknown, not that nothing happened.
  • Arrival timing. A clear is bracketed by the readings either side of it. That interval is not the time a provider started or completed a rollout.
  • No redemption during monitoring. Probes read quota metadata without spending reset credits.

Public observations and probe freshness are available in data.json. Personal and ambiguous clears remain outside that feed, except that a single account’s clear appears as the timing on an announcement it confirms.

Forecast language

The site does not publish a single predicted reset date. Backtests found that point estimates implied more precision than the history supports. Where enough gaps exist, the interface instead reports an empirical conditional rate: among historical gaps already at least as long as the current wait, what fraction ended within the following seven days?

Small comparison sets are labeled. Historical frequency is context, not a promise, causal model, or provider commitment.

Weekday and time-of-day patterns

Codex events with exact timestamps are converted to America/Los_Angeles, including daylight-saving changes, and grouped into four six-hour periods: overnight (00:00–05:59), morning (06:00–11:59), afternoon (12:00–17:59), and evening (18:00–23:59).

The “last 30 days” callout is compared with the complete tracked history. It is descriptive only. Repeated posts may be related, event discovery may be incomplete, and choosing a pattern after seeing the data can exaggerate apparent regularity. The interface therefore calls recent Friday concentration a weak observation rather than a routine.

Machine-readable access and attribution

The complete merged feed is published as JSON. Each event includes a vendor-scoped identifier, announcement time, text, event kind, confidence where applicable, and a source URL. A provider with an own-account probe also carries a probe block (status, last verified time, coverage sentence) and an observations list; only observations cleared for publication appear there. A pair we later withdraw leaves only a marker in that list: its incident key, its id and status: retracted, with no clear, account or time. The homepage also publishes Dataset structured data and a crawlable snapshot of current signals.

When citing this tracker, link both AI Reset Watch and the event’s original source. Do not describe this independent aggregation as an official provider record.

Frequently asked questions

Does AI Reset Watch know when provider systems actually reset?

Only for our own accounts. Probes read the usage windows of the operator’s own Claude Code and Codex accounts, and can say whether a window expired on schedule or cleared early. One account cannot tell a provider reset from one its holder paid for, so an early clear counts as the provider’s reset only with a second witness: another of our accounts clearing the same weekly window within the hour, or a verified provider post saying a reset happened, posted between an hour before and three hours after the clear. Each provider’s coverage sentence says which of our accounts are being read now. Everything else is linked public announcements. No provider telemetry or internal execution time is visible to this site, and no observation here describes any account but ours.

Are the timing patterns predictions?

No. Weekday and time-of-day views are descriptive summaries of tracked public announcement timestamps in Pacific Time. Small samples and missing announcements can create apparent clusters.

Is AI Reset Watch affiliated with Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google?

No. AI Reset Watch is independently maintained and unaffiliated with the providers it tracks.
